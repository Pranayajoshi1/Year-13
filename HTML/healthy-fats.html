What Are Healthy Fats?

Fats are one of the three main macronutrients (fats, proteins and carbohydrates). Healthy fats are found in natural foods and help your body absorb vitamins, protect your organs, and support overall health.

Not all fats are bad! The right kinds of fats can help you perform better, recover faster, and support your fitness goals.

Unsaturated Fats (Healthy)

You'll find these in foods like avocados, nuts, seeds, olive oil, and oily fish. They're the healthy fats your body needs to support your heart, give you energy, and help you stay healthy.

Saturated Fats

These are found in foods like butter, cheese, whole milk, and coconut oil. They aren't something you need to completely avoid, but it's a good idea not to have too much of them.

Trans Fats

Often found in processed and fried foods. Trans fats can increase the risk of heart disease, so it's best to avoid them whenever possible.

Why Does Your Body Need Healthy Fats?

Supports Brain Function

Healthy fats help your brain work properly, making it easier to focus, remember things, and stay alert.

Helps Make Hormones

Your body uses healthy fats to make hormones that help with growth, recovery, and keeping everything running smoothly.

Helps Absorb Vitamins

Some vitamins need fat to work properly, so healthy fats help your body get the most out of the food you eat.

Gives You Lasting Energy

Healthy fats provide steady energy and help keep you feeling full for longer throughout the day.

Best Sources of Healthy Fats

Monounsaturated Fats

  • Avocados
  • Olive oil
  • Nuts (almonds, cashews)
  • Seeds (pumpkin, sunflower)
  • Peanut butter

Polyunsaturated Fats

  • Fatty fish (salmon, tuna)
  • Flaxseeds
  • Chia seeds
  • Walnuts
  • Soybeans

How Much Healthy Fat Do You Need?

The amount of fat you need depends on your age, activity level, and your fitness goals.

A good guideline is that 20–35% of your daily calories should come from healthy fats.

This helps support your energy, hormones, and overall health.

Fat Tips

  • Choose healthy fats most of the time.
  • Try to include a small amount of healthy fat in your meals.
  • Limit fried and highly processed foods.
  • Remember that fats are high in calories, so moderation is key.
