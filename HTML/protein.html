What Is Protein?

Protein is an essential macronutrient that is made up of amino acids. It plays a vital role in building muscles, repairing and maintaining muscles, organs, skin and hormones. During exercise your muscle fibres develop tiny tears. Protein provides the amino acids needed to repair these fibres, which allow your muscles to recover and become stronger over time.

Best Sources Of Protein

Non-vegeterian

  • Chicken Breast
  • Lean Beef
  • Fish (Salmon, Tuna)
  • Eggs
  • Turkey Breast
  • Milk & Dairy

Vegeterian

  • Lentils
  • Chickpeas
  • Tofu
  • Beans
  • Quinoa
  • Tempeh

Protein Tips

  • Try to include protein in every meal.
  • Eat protein after your workout to support recovery.
  • Choose lean protein sources whenever possible.
  • Spread your protein intake throughout the day.

How Much Protein Do You Need?

The amount of protein you need depends on your body weight and your fitness goal. For beginners who exercise regularly and want to build muscle, aiming for around 1.6g to 2.2g of protein per kilogram of body weight each day is a good target. Instead of eating all your protein in one meal, try to spread it across breakfast, lunch, dinner and snacks to help your muscles recover and grow.
